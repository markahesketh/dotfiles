# Operational Persona — Warehouse Supervisor

**One-liner:** Frontline leader responsible for executing the daily plan and coaching teams to meet productivity and quality standards.

## Mission statement
"I exist to execute the daily operational plan and coach my team to meet productivity and quality standards, so that our shift contributes to overall warehouse targets without safety incidents, and I measure success by team performance above standard, quality compliance, and accident-free shifts."

## Organisational context
- 3–7 years' warehouse experience; strong operational knowledge of specific departments (receiving, picking, packing, shipping). Balances floor presence with admin.
- Responsibilities: monitor department performance in real time, conduct observations/training, coach teammates, ensure safety compliance.
- Reports to Operations Manager or Inbound/Outbound Manager. Manages shift operational staff; works with other supervisors, inventory control, safety teams.

## Definition of success
Team performance at/above standard (% of UPH/TPH target) · quality audits passed without major defects · safety compliance, zero incidents · coaching-frequency targets met · team morale and retention maintained.

## Daily activities
- **Pre-shift:** check roster, identify call-outs; build labour plan ("30 people today — 5 here, 10 there, 15 there").
- **Shift start (huddle):** brief team on priorities, safety, targets; assign tasks/zones.
- **First hour (floor):** ensure everyone in position; address equipment/access/questions; monitor start-up productivity.
- **Mid-shift:** track real-time performance via WMS/LM; spot underperformers/bottlenecks; reallocate labour.
- **Hourly:** review department vs. target; coordinate labour-sharing with other supervisors.
- **Continuous coaching:** observe methods, give feedback, address quality, reinforce safety; document sessions. Cadence: real-time updates to ops manager, end-of-shift summary, weekly coaching docs.

## Primary KPIs
- Employee performance % vs. standard (by individual and department)
- Productivity (UPH / TPH for the department)
- Quality audit pass rate (compliance, accuracy checks)
- Safety compliance (behaviour observations, near-miss reporting, incident-free shifts)
- Attendance and retention

## Reporting & decisions
- Power BI: department UPH/TPH, employee performance %, safety incidents, coaching frequency.
- WMS/LM: real-time productivity by associate, dept vs. standard, task completion, quality audit results.
- Teams chat / radio: real-time comms with supervisors, ops management, support (inventory, maintenance).
- Decisions: labour allocation at shift start, who to coach/retrain, when to escalate equipment/system issues.

## Pain points (with impact)
- **Absenteeism disrupts planning:** plan built on expected headcount, then 5–10 call out → run lean or pull labour from other departments. → Pre-shift planning wasted; reactive decisions; morale and targets suffer.
- **Productivity vs. quality/safety:** constant pressure to hit targets while maintaining quality and safety; understaffed → temptation to cut corners. → Caught between competing priorities; quality/safety issues when productivity over-emphasised.
- **Limited time for coaching:** firefighting (absenteeism, equipment, systems) eats most time. → Coaching becomes reactive; underperformers don't improve; high performers unrecognised.
- **Technology misuse/distractions:** phones/wearables on the floor create safety and productivity risks; policy enforcement inconsistent. → Safety incidents up; time spent policing instead of coaching.
- **Fragmented performance visibility:** real-time data exists in WMS/LM but must be actively checked; no proactive alerts. → Issues found late in shift; time on reports instead of floor coaching.
- **Cross-shift communication gaps:** handover is verbal/manual notes; decisions and anomalies not systematically captured. → Each shift starts from scratch; recurring issues re-discovered.

## Overarching need
"To lead a high-performing, engaged team that consistently meets targets while maintaining safety and quality, without burning out from constant firefighting."
