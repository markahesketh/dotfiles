# Operational Persona — Inventory Manager

**One-liner:** Senior inventory specialist responsible for maintaining inventory accuracy and availability across the warehouse.

## Mission statement
"I exist to ensure inventory accuracy and availability across the warehouse, so that operators can fulfil orders without disruption and blocked inventory & shortages are minimised, and I measure success by low blocked-inventory rates, high cycle-count accuracy and zero controllable shortfalls."

## Organisational context
- 5–10 years' warehouse experience, often promoted from analyst or cycle counter. Deep WMS and inventory-process knowledge.
- Responsibilities: investigate discrepancies, manage cycle counting, monitor aging/blocked inventory, coordinate adjustments, give quality feedback.
- Reports to Operations Manager or General Manager. Manages cycle counters, quality auditors, and clerks (reporting/system tasks).

## Definition of success
Blocked inventory below 0.3% target · order shortages minimised · cycle-count accuracy >98% · aging inventory flagged and resolved proactively · discrepancies resolved within 24–48h · location accuracy maintained.

## Daily activities
- **Pre-shift:** review overnight reports on blocked/aging inventory, shortages, location accuracy.
- **Morning review:** Power BI for blocked inventory and shortfalls; export for deeper analysis; review overnight discrepancy reports.
- **9am coordination:** assign cycle-count priorities with supervisors; discuss overnight issues; coordinate with ops on blocked locations.
- **Continuous:** investigate discrepancies (often via Teams chat), track missing pallets, decide adjust vs. keep searching.
- **Afternoon:** feed root-cause findings to operations; update SOPs; coordinate custom reports with analytics.
- **End-of-shift:** resolution status, handover notes, KPI tracking. Cadence: daily ops updates, weekly KPI reviews, monthly trends.

## Primary KPIs
- Blocked inventory % (lost / wrong location / quality hold)
- Order shortfalls (controllable, preventable)
- Cycle-count accuracy (first-count, no adjustments)
- Aging inventory (SKUs static >5–7 days)
- Location accuracy (physical vs. system match)
- Discrepancy resolution time

## Reporting & decisions
- Power BI: blocked inventory, cuts, aging, location accuracy, second-count performance.
- WMS: 30-day transaction history, allocation status, location audits, quality holds.
- Email subs: daily blocked-inventory alerts, shortage reports, cycle-count results.
- Decisions: what to prioritise, when to trigger an adjustment vs. keep searching, how to allocate count resources, when to escalate system issues.

## Pain points (with impact)
- **Data & visibility:** morning means multiple Power BI reports, Excel exports, cross-referencing WMS screens — no single inventory-health view. → 30–45 min gathering data before real work; issues found reactively.
- **System limitations:** WMS retains only 30 days; older data needs slow archives; false allocations with no tasks; missing fields need manual workarounds / IT. → Root-cause is slow and often incomplete; historical trend analysis nearly impossible.
- **Manual, tedious processes:** cycle counting is labour-intensive; investigations are manual tracing; adjustments need multiple approvals/entries. → Time goes to reactive firefighting; high human-error risk.
- **Resource constraints:** small team vs. warehouse size; daily ops vs. projects/audits/improvements compete. → Improvements deprioritised when daily issues hit.
- **Communication gaps:** translating data for non-analytical ops staff is hard; feedback loops are slow. → Same issues recur because root causes aren't addressed.
- **Aging inventory management:** lifecycle decisions reactive, only at near-capacity. → Congested pick areas, longer travel, rushed clearance.

## Overarching need
"To maintain perfect inventory accuracy with minimal manual effort, so operations never experiences a shortage due to inventory issues."
