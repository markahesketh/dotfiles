# Forrester TEI Reference: DexoryView Benefits & Moderation Guide

Source: Forrester TEI Study, commissioned by Dexory, December 2024.
Composite org: Global 3PL, $2B revenue, 50,000 sqm warehouses, 100 employees/warehouse, 3 customers/warehouse, $10M revenue/warehouse/year.

---

## TEI Headline Metrics (3-year, risk-adjusted PV)
- ROI: 219%
- NPV: $2.96M
- Benefits PV: $4.30M
- Costs PV: $1.35M
- Payback: <6 months

---

## Benefit A: Reduced Stockholding Cost — PV $1.10M (26% of total)
**TEI assumption:** 30% reduction in stockholding time (10 weeks → 7 weeks). Average stockholding cost $9M/warehouse/year. One-time benefit per warehouse in Year 1 of deployment.
**Key questionnaire inputs:** Q5.1 (annual holding cost), Q5.2 (avg stockholding weeks), Q5.3 (replenishment frequency)
**Moderation flags:**
- The 30% reduction is the headline from one high-performing customer — treat as an upper bound. 15–20% is a more defensible starting estimate for a prospect.
- This benefit is entirely dependent on the prospect being a 3PL or multi-customer site. A single-occupier warehouse gets almost none of this benefit.
- "Stockholding cost" in the TEI includes revenue opportunity from turning space over faster — this is really a revenue uplift dressed as a cost saving. Flag this when presenting to finance audiences as it will require revenue assumptions.
- Risk-adjusted downward 15% in TEI. For prospects with less confident baselines, consider 25–30% downward adjustment.

## Benefit B: Fewer Lost Pallets — PV $915K (21% of total)
**TEI assumption:** 5% of pallets lost/year baseline; 30% reduction with Dexory; $1,000/pallet value; investigation time reduced from 24 hrs → 30 min; 9,000 pallets/warehouse.
**Key questionnaire inputs:** Q4.1 (pallets lost/year), Q4.2 (inventory write-off value), Q4.3 (investigation time), Q1.2 (pallet locations)
**Moderation flags:**
- The 5% baseline loss rate is high — it came from an aviation cargo interviewee who noted 500–600 pallets lost/year. Many well-run warehouses will be 1–2%, not 5%.
- If a prospect doesn't know their loss rate, this benefit should be flagged as "requires validation" rather than estimated upward.
- The $1,000/pallet value is also a composite assumption — it needs to match the actual product mix.
- The 30% reduction in lost pallets is well-evidenced by the 60% UTL drop at the aviation customer — use 25–35% as a credible range.
- Risk-adjusted downward 10% in TEI — likely insufficient for prospects with poor baseline data quality.

## Benefit C: Automated Cycle Counting Efficiencies — PV $867K (20% of total)
**TEI assumption:** 5 cycle counters/warehouse pre-Dexory; 80% reduction (down to 1); $50,000 fully burdened annual salary each.
**Key questionnaire inputs:** Q2.1 (FTEs for cycle counting), Q2.2 (fully burdened cost/counter)
**Moderation flags:**
- This is the most reliable and directly calculable benefit — it scales directly from Q2.1 and Q2.2 answers.
- The "reallocation not redundancy" framing is important: Forrester assumes staff are redeployed, not made redundant. This is the ethically safer and more credible claim. Don't model headcount reduction unless the prospect explicitly confirms it.
- 80% reduction is the TEI upper bound. A more defensible range is 60–75% in Year 1 as change management takes time. Ask whether the site runs day AND night shifts (Q2.1 specifies this) — night shift counters are harder to redeploy.
- Risk-adjusted downward 10% in TEI.

## Benefit D: Reduced Fines from Improved Fulfillment — PV $693K (16% of total)
**TEI assumption:** 100,000 orders/warehouse/year; accuracy improves 99.5% → 99.9%; $400 fine per unfulfilled order.
**Key questionnaire inputs:** Q6.1 (orders/year), Q6.2 (OTIF rate), Q6.3 (cost per fulfilment error), Q1.7 (current inventory accuracy)
**Moderation flags:**
- The $400/order fine is specific to 3PL retail contracts with major retailers (e.g., Tesco, Amazon). This does NOT apply to owner-operated warehouses or B2B distributors.
- The 0.4pp accuracy improvement (99.5% → 99.9%) sounds small but multiplied by 100,000 orders becomes 400 fewer errors. The maths is correct but the starting assumption of 99.5% accuracy is already quite good — prospects below 99% will see much higher uplift.
- If the prospect doesn't receive fines/chargebacks, this benefit should be reframed as "cost of quality/error resolution" using Q6.3 and Q6.4 instead.
- Risk-adjusted downward 10% in TEI.

## Benefit E: Reduced Racking Damage — PV $264K (6% of total)
**TEI assumption:** 9,000 pallets/warehouse; $113/rack repair cost; 6% reduction in racking damage.
**Key questionnaire inputs:** Q7.3 (annual racking spend), Q7.4 (cost per damaged bay), Q7.2 (hours spent inspecting)
**Moderation flags:**
- 6% racking damage reduction is a relatively modest and therefore credible number — less likely to be challenged.
- This benefit is highly dependent on current inspection frequency (Q7.1). If the prospect already runs daily inspections, the marginal benefit is lower.
- The compliance/safety angle (Q7.6) is an unquantified benefit in the TEI but can be a powerful narrative for safety-conscious operators — don't ignore it.
- Risk-adjusted downward 10% in TEI.

---

## Benefit F: Empty Location Checks — PV $216K (5% of total)
**TEI assumption:** 1 FTE dedicated to empty location checks; 40 hrs/week saved; $24/hour.
**Key questionnaire inputs:** Q8.1 (hours/week on empty location checks)
**Moderation flags:**
- This is a small but very clean benefit — directly driven by Q8.1.
- Many warehouses won't have a dedicated person for this; instead it'll be partial FTE time. That's fine — use Q8.1 hours × hourly rate.
- Risk-adjusted downward 10% in TEI.

---

## Benefit G: Reduced Audit Fees — PV $254K (6% of total)
**TEI assumption:** $60,000 internal audit cost saved/warehouse/year; $20,000 external audit fee; 10% external fee saved.
**Key questionnaire inputs:** Q3.1 (frequency of full counts), Q3.2 (days of halted operations), Q3.3 (total audit cost)
**Moderation flags:**
- The $60,000 internal cost saving is plausible for large 3PLs but may be overstated for smaller or well-automated sites.
- Q3.3 asks for total audit cost directly — use that figure rather than the TEI assumption where possible.
- The "no operational downtime" claim (Q3.2 × daily revenue) can be very compelling for high-throughput sites.
- Risk-adjusted downward 15% in TEI — higher than other benefits, signalling Forrester's own uncertainty.

## Unquantified Benefits (narrative use only — do NOT model financially)
- Improved H&S and reduced forklift use
- Employee retention (TEI cites 99.4% retention — extreme outlier, don't cite this directly)
- No overtime / temp labour during peak periods (this CAN be estimated if prospect has peak labour data)
- Reduced training / supervision
- Cleaner warehouse and equipment

## Cost Summary (for context when building ROI model)
- License fee: $12,000/month/warehouse ($144,000/year) — TEI risk-adjusts UP by 10% to $158,400
- Setup/implementation: $20,000 one-time Dexory fee + ~$67,500 internal labour per warehouse
- Ongoing internal optimisation: ~$59,000/year/warehouse (ops engineer + tech specialist, 20% time each)
- Training: Negligible (~$400 per warehouse)
- Total 3-year cost PV: $1.35M for 3 warehouses

**Pricing note for skill users:** Dexory pricing varies by site configuration. For a basic racked warehouse, ~$12,000/month is the reference point. Block stacks, mixed configurations, or larger warehouses will differ. Always get a pricing range from the commercial team before building a prospect-specific model.
